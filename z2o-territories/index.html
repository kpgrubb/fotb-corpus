Executive summary

Of 23 opportunity hypotheses, none survives as a zero-to-one territory for a bank. One survives as an adjacency worth a measured test. Thirteen survive as features of products the bank already runs, each with a kill test it can run on its own data inside a quarter. Nine die. The ten-to-fifteen list the sponsor asked for does not exist on this evidence, and this page says so rather than padding it.

Why the seeds narrowed

Three patterns repeat. The pain is real but it is income, price or policy, not a missing product (caregiving-15, rupture-20, domicile-24). Where a product gap exists, someone already fills it or already failed in it: Rocket owns the home-purchase chain and lost money (domicile-03), CareCredit finances care at 290,000 points of care (rupture-01), five couples-money apps failed without a disclosed user count (partnerships-01). And the bank's one edge is the account it already holds, which supports a retention feature, not a company.

The framework result

Framework A (money moment) is the life-territory frame; it says where to look but no large winner except SoFi documents a moment as its origin (framework-14). Framework B (value-chain position) survives and generates: Affirm and Klarna began at checkout on a bank balance sheet (framework-13). Framework C is a risk label. A fourth frame the sponsor did not list, cost structure, distribution and regulatory position, explains most outcomes (framework-01, 02, 03, 09, 10), and most of those edges belong to challengers.

The one adjacency

Death and estate, as survivor navigation added to the bank's own account-settlement process (transfer-08). Planning is already bank-distributed (transfer-02) and navigation has funded incumbents (transfer-01). It turns on one unmeasured number: whether heirs keep a dead customer's money at the bank. Capital One's own estate-settlement records can answer it in weeks. Reliability grade C.

What the sponsor brief got wrong

The bank does offer small-business banking; it closed Brex in April 2026; Discover brought three payment networks; it held and sold Discover's home loans in 2025, telling the OCC it could not compete with nonbank lenders; a found-money product already exists (franchise-01 to 07). The 360 Savings settlement narrows the "trust from holding assets" claim (franchise-09), and the 2020 OCC order contradicts "decades of risk capability" on technology risk (franchise-10).

What to do with this: run the thirteen kill tests before ideating, most of which are internal data queries; measure heir retention to settle the one adjacency; and re-aim the next hunt at positions in flows and ownable edges, asking which life moments they touch, rather than the reverse. Treat an empty field as a negative result: in partnerships, five failed attempts are evidence about the economics, not an opening.

The framework memo in brief

Use the eight life territories as the map and the corpus as the demand evidence. Do not use them to pick what to pursue. Run every candidate through three filters in order: an ownable edge on the fourth frame, a position the bank can occupy and fund (Framework B), then a risk label (Framework C).

Gaps and next research

No opportunity in the sweep has willingness-to-pay or switching evidence. Most kill tests are internal data queries: heir retention from estate-settlement records, POA request volumes and attrition, the declined-overdraft segment's size and losses, joint-account conversions and disputes, refund-opened account primacy, large-balance outflow destinations, the subscription manager's retention effect, remittance outflows in the base, and whether Card already uses deposit data.

External steps: a probability-panel item on willingness to switch (commissioned or proposed for a Fed SHED wave); HMDA loan-cost comparison by lender type; Huntington's Caregiver Banking uptake; Fifth Third's Trust & Will retention; a CFPB complaint-narrative search on deceased-customer servicing; a Fiscal Service request on where 2026's former paper-check refunds went. Dates to watch: Treasury's Saver's Match listing criteria (January 2027), the customer-due-diligence proposal under EO 14406 (mid-November 2026), Section 1033's replacement rule, the Fourth Circuit 360 Savings appeal (briefs November to December 2026), and Flex's Utah charter decision.

Method and limits

Five stages on 26 October 2026 with about sixty agents: scout (hypotheses homed under the life territories), mine (read-only extraction of demand evidence from the September corpus), research (two web slices per territory, supply and right-to-win, with disconfirmation mandatory), compile (one agent per territory merging, pruning and re-verifying every cited number against the fetched page), and stress-test (one adversary per territory). A merge script validated every row against the taxonomy, the opportunity list and the controlled vocabularies before writing the final table. Every compile corrected numbers that the research pass had attached to real URLs, including one table in the September corpus read a column off. Limits: demand is unmeasured for every opportunity; several Capital One facts are opaque from outside; regulatory status was checked as of October 2026 and moves; search budgets were thirty per slice, so absence of a named player is not proof of absence.
